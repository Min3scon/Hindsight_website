## Q1. Which Windows 11 / Windows 10 end-of-support annoyances drive the most recurring complaints in 2025-2026, and which are NOT well solved by free tools?

### Takeaway
The biggest 2025-2026 complaint themes are forced AI/Copilot, the oversized new Start menu, slow File Explorer and right-click menus, and cumulative updates that break gaming. Microsoft is now walking several of these back itself, and free open-source debloaters with tens of thousands of GitHub stars already cover the "remove stuff" job, so building around those themes is crowded and shrinking. The clearest *new* gap is that **Microsoft removed Windows 11's built-in local PC-to-PC migration in October 2026 and pushed users to OneDrive-based Windows Backup**. That lands while a large Windows 10 holdout base is still moving to new hardware, and no strong free tool fills the gap.

### Inferences
- **Debloat and de-AI is a large but low-willingness-to-pay audience.** Free tools with more than 60k stars, plus Microsoft's own rollback, make a paid "remove Copilot" app a weak bet unless it adds something the scripts lack: a signed, reversible GUI for non-technical users with drift detection after feature updates. I could not confirm that updates re-enable debloated settings (see Gaps).
- **Start menu and Explorer fixes are being "sherlocked."** Microsoft is shipping Start customization and a faster context menu in 2026, and Start11 v3 (US$10) already owns paid Start customization. A new entrant would struggle.
- **PC migration is the most time-sensitive opening.** The removal (October 2026), the OneDrive and Microsoft-account requirement, and the remaining Windows 10 base (about 28% globally, about 48% in Germany) create a fresh, news-driven need just as people buy holiday PCs. Local-account users are explicitly locked out of Microsoft's path.
- Updates that break gaming recur roughly every 2-3 months in 2026 (January, February, August). This points to a "what changed on my PC since it was fine?" tool that correlates update/driver install history with performance regressions and offers rollback guidance.

## Q2. What do gamers and PC builders struggle with repeatedly?

### Takeaway
The recurring gamer pains are (1) **stutter**: shader compilation stutter, Windows-update regressions, and DPC latency that people chase for months without diagnosis; (2) **bloated vendor software** such as Armoury Crate (about 4 GB installer) and iCUE (3.54 GB installed); (3) **manual HDR and display toggling per game**; and (4) **text fringing on OLED monitors**. Gamers demonstrably pay for small utilities on Steam (Lossless Scaling: US$6.99, about 36.5k reviews). Hardware-control categories (RGB, fans, monitoring) are a poor fit for a solo developer: they need low-level drivers, and those drivers now get flagged by Windows Defender.

### Inferences
- **A stutter *interpreter* is the gamer-side gap.** Data-capture tools are free (PresentMon, CapFrameX, LatencyMon), but nobody turns frametime, DPC/ISR, background-process, and update/driver-history data into a plain-English verdict ("your spikes line up with nvlddmkm.sys DPCs, which started after driver X / KB Y"). SCSKiller's star velocity shows how hungry this audience is for stutter fixes, though SCSKiller itself is free.
- **Per-game display profiles** (HDR, refresh rate, resolution, color profile, audio device, power plan) are a real but cheap category ($5-10), with free OSS options. They could be sold on Steam where discovery exists.
- **Avoid RGB, fan control, and sensor monitoring** as a solo product. They need SMBus/EC access through kernel drivers. The 2025 WinRing0 Defender flags hit even the category leaders, new drivers need EV/attestation signing, and free OSS leaders already exist.
- **OLED text rendering is high-demand but high-risk.** It is the #1 open PowerToys request (821 thumbs-up), but a fix means injecting into processes and hooking DirectWrite/GDI. That brings AV false-positive, anti-cheat, and browser-renderer problems, so it is not a days-to-weeks project.

### Gaps

- No 2026 survey of how many PC builders migrate versus clean-install; no hard data on demand for BIOS/driver management tools (not researched in depth because the search budget ran out).

## Q5. Platform and distribution risks (constraint check)

### Takeaway
Distribution is cheaper than ever: the **Microsoft Store is free for individual developers and accepts Win32 apps**, and Azure Artifact Signing costs about US$9.99/month. But **SmartScreen reputation still has to be earned**, Artifact Signing for individuals is limited to the US and Canada, and **tweak, debloat, and hardware-access tools routinely get flagged by Defender and AV products**. Microsoft also regularly "sherlocks" popular utility features.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft Store registration for individual developers is free (previously US$19). It requires ID and selfie verification and accepts Win32/WPF/WinForms/Electron apps. Developers can use their own commerce and keep 100%, or pay Microsoft 15% on apps — [Windows Developer Blog, 2025-09-10](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2025/09/10/free-developer-registration-for-individual-developers-on-microsoft-store/); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/9/microsoft-finally-drops-microsoft-store-publishing-fee-for-individual-developers-globally). A later report says company accounts became free too — [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/microsoft-makes-company-developer-accounts-free-for-the-microsoft-store/).
- Azure Artifact Signing (renamed from Trusted Signing): the Basic tier is about US$9.99/month for 5,000 signatures (seen on one regional page; verify) — [Azure pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/pricing/details/artifact-signing/); [LavX](https://news.lavx.hu/article/microsoft-s-artifact-signing-rebrands-and-goes-ga-a-fully-managed-code-signing-service-for-windows-developers). Individuals are limited to the USA and Canada; developers elsewhere use OV certificates at about US$150-300/yr. Signing gives only base SmartScreen reputation, not instant trust — [Microsoft Learn code-signing options](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/code-signing-options).

### Inferences
- **Prefer user-mode-only designs** (no kernel drivers, no DLL injection into other processes, no undocumented shell hooks). That minimizes AV flags and update breakage. Migration (file copy plus winget), diagnostics (ETW/PresentMon, which needs admin but no driver), and display profiles (documented DisplayConfig APIs) all qualify.
- **Ship through the Microsoft Store (free, Store-signed, so no SmartScreen hurdle) or Steam (for gamer tools), and also sell direct** with Artifact Signing if US/Canada-based. A Store listing removes most of the signing and SmartScreen cost for a solo developer.
- **Plan for sherlock risk:** pick problems Microsoft is moving *away* from solving (local migration without OneDrive) rather than toward.

### Gaps
- I did not verify current Microsoft Store policy on system-tweaking apps (whether apps that modify OS settings or registry pass certification).
- No data on Steam's acceptance criteria for non-game utilities in 2026.

## Top 5 candidate product ideas from this angle

### 1. Local "Move to New PC" migration tool (no OneDrive, no Microsoft account)
- **Problem:** Microsoft removed Windows 11's local PC-to-PC transfer in October 2026 (KB5127648). Windows Backup needs a Microsoft account and OneDrive (5 GB free, paid for real data volumes), and it reinstalls nothing automatically. Meanwhile the Windows 10 base (about 28% globally mid-2026, about 48% in Germany in January 2026) still has to move to new hardware.
- **Evidence strength:** **Strong on timing and need** (Windows Latest 2026-11-02, PCWorld, HelpDeskGeek). **Moderate on willingness to pay:** an established paid category exists (PCmover, O&O), and Intel/Laplink run campaigns around it. There is essentially no open-source competitor (top GitHub hit has 1 star).
- **Competitors and prices:** Laplink PCmover US$59.95, with complaints that it is slow, misses apps, licenses one machine pair only, and has risked file loss. O&O Migration Kit from US$69.95. EaseUS Todo PCTrans and Zinstall exist, prices unverified.
- **Differentiated angle:** "Honest migration" at US$19-29 one-time with a *reusable* license (households and refurbishers). Transfer over LAN, Wi-Fi direct, or USB drive; Windows 10 to 11; works with **local accounts**. Move user folders, browser profiles, Wi-Fi profiles, fonts, and per-app settings for the top ~50 apps. **Rebuild the app list through winget** instead of copying binaries, which is safer and fixes PCmover's "apps didn't transfer" complaint. Offer a dry-run report, checksum verification, and resume. A "Windows 10 → new PC" landing page fits the post-ESU news cycle.
- **Build difficulty:** Medium. File copy, verification, and the winget export/import core are a few days of work. The per-app settings long tail and the trust/QA burden (data-loss liability) take longer. Fully user-mode, Store-eligible.

### 2. "Stutter Doctor": one-click game stutter diagnosis with a plain-English verdict
- **Problem:** Gamers chase stutter for months ("1 year of persistent stutter... all fixes exhausted"). Current workflows chain three free tools (CapFrameX/PresentMon, LatencyMon) with expert interpretation. Windows updates caused gaming regressions in January, February, and August 2026.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong on pain: forum threads, repeated update regressions, and SCSKiller reaching 4,662 stars in about 7 weeks. Strong that gamers pay for small Steam utilities (Lossless Scaling at US$6.99 with about 36.5k reviews; about 2.7M copies is an unverified estimate).
- **Competitors and prices:** CapFrameX (free), Intel PresentMon (free), LatencyMon (free for home use; pro edition price unverified), SCSKiller (free, shader precompile only).
- **Differentiated angle:** Record a 60-120 second session and correlate frametime spikes with DPC/ISR culprits (ETW), background processes and overlays, shader-cache events, thermals/clocks, *and* the Windows update and driver install timeline ("spikes began after KB5074109 / driver 5xx.xx; NVIDIA recommends uninstalling"). Output a ranked fix list and a before/after comparison. Sell on Steam for US$5-10, optionally with a paid "deep report".
- **Build difficulty:** Medium-hard. PresentMon is open source and ETW is user-mode with admin rights, so no kernel driver is needed. The interpretation rules take iteration; accuracy and credibility are the main risk.

### 3. Per-game display and HDR profile automator
- **Problem:** Windows still makes users toggle HDR, refresh rate, resolution, and color settings manually per game or video. Windows' own auto-HDR switching and per-game Auto HDR only partly help.
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate. Free tools keep getting press (Smart HDR Manager, Wccftech and Windows Central 2026, 338 stars in about a month; AutoActions 792 stars), which signals demand but also free competition.
- **Competitors and prices:** Smart HDR Manager, AutoActions, and DisplayProfileManager are free OSS. DisplayFusion Pro (US$34 / US$49 household / US$39.99 Steam) has monitor profiles and triggers but is a heavy general-purpose tool.
- **Differentiated angle:** A gamer-first "launch-triggered profiles" app that sets HDR, refresh rate, resolution, ICC profile, audio output device, power plan, and Game Mode or process priority per executable, restoring everything on exit. Add OLED-care extras (auto-hide taskbar, dim desktop) and Steam/Epic/Xbox library auto-detection. Steam, US$4.99-7.99.
- **Build difficulty:** Low-medium. Documented DisplayConfig and advanced-color APIs plus process watching; days to an MVP. The risk is low price and the chance Microsoft adds it natively.

### 4. Per-monitor workspaces (window-management pack for unsolved PowerToys requests)
- **Problem:** Several of the most-voted PowerToys requests have stayed unsolved for 5-7 years: independent virtual desktops per monitor (338 thumbs-up), maximize within zone (335), dock/undock layouts (293), focus-steal prevention (169), and moving a window with you across desktops (140).
- **Evidence strength:** Strong on demand (GitHub votes accumulated since 2019). Moderate on willingness to pay: DisplayFusion and Groupy sell, but tiling fans favor free komorebi and GlazeWM.
- **Competitors and prices:** DisplayFusion Pro US$34, Groupy 2 US$9.99, Start11/Fences via Object Desktop US$49.99; free: PowerToys FancyZones/Workspaces, komorebi (15.4k stars), GlazeWM (13k), and tiny new per-monitor desktop projects (under 10 stars).
- **Differentiated angle:** macOS-Spaces-style *per-monitor* workspaces built by showing and hiding window sets per monitor (avoiding the fragile undocumented virtual-desktop COM APIs). Laptop dock/undock auto-layouts and a focus-steal guard are included. US$12-19 one-time.
- **Build difficulty:** Medium-hard. Window-management edge cases (DPI, UWP/WinUI windows, fullscreen games, Explorer restarts) and breakage on Windows updates. User-mode only.

### 5. "Set-and-forget" Windows de-annoyance with drift detection and one-click undo
- **Problem:** Persistent annoyances: Copilot and AI features, Store results in Search, OneDrive and Edge auto-start, ads and recommendations in Start, and the slow right-click menu caused by shell extensions. Non-technical users fear running PowerShell debloat scripts.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong audience size (winutil 66k stars, Win11Debloat 63k, Winhance 15k). **Weak willingness to pay**, because free tools are excellent and Microsoft is itself removing Copilot surfaces (November-December 2026). Re-enablement after updates is unconfirmed.
- **Competitors and prices:** All major ones are free (winutil, Win11Debloat, Winhance, O&O ShutUp10++, Optimizer, Flyoobe, RemoveWindowsAI). Microsoft PC Manager is free.
- **Differentiated angle:** A signed, Store-distributed GUI that explains each toggle and snapshots settings so everything is reversible. It **monitors for drift after each cumulative or feature update** and notifies the user (needs validation). Bundle a "context-menu doctor" that times each shell extension and disables the slow ones, plus a "what changed since my PC was fine" update and driver timeline. US$9-15.
- **Build difficulty:** Low-medium technically, but **high AV false-positive risk** (winutil and ExplorerPatcher history) and Store-policy uncertainty for system-tweak apps. Lowest-confidence pick; best as a feature of #1 or #2 rather than standalone.

*Honorable mentions:* (a) **OLED text renderer**: biggest single vote count (821 thumbs-up), but it needs process injection and DirectWrite hooking, which is too risky for a days-to-weeks build. (b) **Global mic-mute hotkey with overlay**: PowerToys deprecated Video Conference Mute (#37126, 82 thumbs-up; #38604, 62 thumbs-up, open). Trivial to build, but likely only US$3-5 and free alternatives probably exist (not verified). (c) **Windhawk/PowerToys config sync and export across PCs**: recurring requests (#195, #462, PowerToys #3381); could be folded into idea #1.
