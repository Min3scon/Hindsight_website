# Sell Into the Gap Blender 5 Broke

Build a Grease Pencil v3 "stroke-to-mesh" add-on for Blender 5.x first, sell it on Superhive (formerly Blender Market) at about **$24** with Superhive's paid 12-month Support Period renewals, and mirror it on Gumroad. Of eight finalists drawn from roughly 30 candidate products across the research, it is the only one that clears all five of your gates at once: Superhive is an established marketplace whose buyers already pay $25–$79 for tools; the AI agent can build and test the add-on end to end in this Linux container through the `bpy` package; it needs no server, license backend or website; and it fills a documented gap, because Blender 5.0's API removals affected **116 add-ons, about 30% of which were still partly broken a couple of releases later**, while the only dedicated paid tool in this niche (G-Mesh) stops at Blender 4.0 with "no plans for future updates." The catch is revenue: one forum creator puts a Superhive add-on from a seller with no audience at roughly **$50 a month**, and Superhive's flagship sales average only **$239–$375 per creator**, so treat this as a cheap first rung that proves the build-test-list pipeline and starts a catalog, not a windfall. A slotted-Actions animation pack on the same channel is the pre-committed fallback; a Unity 6 Render Graph tool has stronger demand evidence but cannot be fully tested here; and the highest-upside category, $5–$10 Windows utilities on Steam, fails the testability gate and depends on a hit. Because research tools could not open any marketplace page or Reddit, every figure below comes from search-result snippets, so the first validation step is a live check of Superhive before any code is written.

## Your five constraints rule out the indie formats with the best revenue records

The products with the strongest documented solo revenue are precisely the ones your constraints exclude. Freemium Chrome extensions reach a first sale fastest, and Easy Folders reached **$3.7K MRR and $42K lifetime within six months** ([small-start.com](https://small-start.com/en/cases/global-6-months-post-launch-my-chrome-extension-has-h/)); native Mac apps sold direct, such as BoltAI, average **$15K–$30K a month** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-going-viral-on-x-helped-this-solopreneur-reach-15k-mo-with-his-native-ai-apps-ZBbFBKrpwqZQkQsy7Bcs)). Both sell through the seller's own checkout. The developer storefronts that do offer discovery lack built-in payments: the Chrome Web Store has had **no native payments since 2021** ([MicroGaps](https://www.microgaps.com/gaps/chrome-extension-monetization-sdk-gap)), the VS Code Marketplace has no paid listings ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sell-vscode-extensions)), Raycast has no paid-extension mechanism ([unsubbed](https://unsubbed.pages.dev/tools/raycast-extensions/)), and Obsidian leaves payment to the developer ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/paid-plugin-market-and-how-to-solve-unmaintained-plugins/109137)). Base rates justify leaning on a store's search box rather than on launches: across **8,817 indie products with payment-verified revenue, 54% earn $0 a month and only 12.1% clear $1,000** ([Scoutr](https://www.scoutr.dev/blog/indie-startup-revenue-report)), and the usual cause is missing distribution, not bad code.

The "no paid infrastructure, low support" gate removes most server-backed products. Discord requires paid bot features to go through its Premium Apps store, which demands a verified app owned by a developer team in the US, UK or EU ([Discord docs](https://docs.discord.com/developers/monetization/enabling-monetization)), and a bot needs always-on hosting. Shopify lets developers keep **100% of the first $1M** ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)), but that means running a hosted app whose support and ad costs eat the margin ([AdsX](https://adsx.com/blog/how-much-shopify-app-developers-make)). Atlassian Forge's 0% fee up to $1M looks generous, yet the opportunity depends on Connect apps reaching end-of-support in **Q4 2026**, which is now, and no specific replacement target was identified ([Isogun Labs](https://isogunlabs.com/blog/atlassian-marketplace-2026/)). The testability gate then pushes down Windows-only GUI utilities for the Microsoft Store and Steam, which cannot run in this container, and Fab code plugins, which must compile against every declared Unreal version with newer MSVC toolchains where a single failure rejects the whole submission ([Pixelsdesign](https://www.pixelsdesign.it/blog/unreal-engine-plugin-multiple-engine-versions.html)). Godot's official store does not sell paid assets yet ([GameFromScratch](https://gamefromscratch.com/the-godot-asset-store-is-live/)), so it is a watch item rather than a channel.

Age and identity checks point to 18+ in practice, because almost every payout path runs through a government ID and a legally binding agreement. Gumroad is the only marketplace with a documented path for minors: sellers can start at 13, but a parent or guardian must be added for payouts, and the path is **US-only** ([Gumroad Help](https://gumroad.com/help/article/260-your-payout-settings-page)). Apple requires the local age of majority ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/128800)), Stripe said in 2022 that 13–17-year-olds need guardian sign-off before funds reach a bank ([Stripe on X](https://x.com/stripe/status/1546835126302097408?lang=en)), and Unity requires two-factor authentication for payouts, paid in USD ([Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/AssetStorePayouts.html)). The research found **no stated minimum age for Superhive, Unity, JetBrains, Steam, the Microsoft Store or itch.io**, so a young adult with an ID and a bank account in their own name hits no documented blocker, while a minor should plan on Gumroad or a parent-held account.

**The slotted-Actions pack is the fallback because it shares #1's channel, buyers and test harness.** Blender 5.0 removed the legacy Action API in favour of layered, slotted Actions ([Blender 5.0 Python API notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/python_api/)), and Loom's render-selected-keyframes feature broke as a direct result ([blender-loom #124](https://github.com/p2or/blender-loom/issues/124)). No dedicated paid slotted-Actions tool surfaced, so a pack combining a slot-aware action library, retargeting between slots, batch keyframe operations and render-selected-keys would face little direct competition. Its code is simpler and more deterministic than #1's, which makes it even easier to test, but it ranks second because its demand evidence is one broken free add-on plus the general breakage survey, with no abandoned paid product proving that buyers pay for this job.

**The Unity tool has the best demand evidence and the worst testability of the top three.** Unity staff said URP's Compatibility Mode exists "so asset providers have time to create RenderGraph versions of their assets" and that Unity plans to wind it down over Unity 6's lifetime ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/introduction-of-render-graph-in-the-universal-render-pipeline-urp/930355?page=22)). Assets such as UImGui fail unless Render Graph is disabled ([uimgui #74](https://github.com/psydack/uimgui/issues/74)), and Unity's own Render Graph samples were reported broken on 6000.3.17f1 ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/unitys-urp-rendergraph-samples-are-broken-6000-3-17f1/1723307)). Prices support $25–45, with vHierarchy 2 at **$35 and 133 reviews** ([Asset Store](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/tools/utilities/vhierarchy-2-253397)) and Odin Inspector at **$55 with about 790 ratings** ([Asset Store reviews](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/tools/utilities/odin-inspector-and-serializer-89041/reviews)). The weakness is verification: a static C# scanner can be unit-tested with .NET on Linux, but proving that generated `RecordRenderGraph` scaffolding compiles and renders requires a real Unity editor, and the notes never established that a licensed editor can run headless in this container.

**JetBrains has the best economics but no proven niche.** JetBrains takes 15% and handles licensing, renewals and refunds ([JetBrains docs](https://plugins.jetbrains.com/docs/marketplace/revenue-sharing-and-fees.html)); its marketplace serves **3M+ monthly downloads across 11,500+ plugins** ([JetBrains](https://plugins.jetbrains.com/developers)), and a third-party scan counted only about 404 paid plugins, unverified ([GitHub scan](https://github.com/dhyabi2/jetbrains-marketplace-scan)). Gradle builds and headless plugin tests suit this container. The notes found no specific unmet IDE need, so pairing JetBrains with supply-chain pain is my own synthesis. That pain is real and worsening: the ChainDrop worm hit **444 npm packages in under four hours** ([Microsoft Security](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/08/04/chaindrop-supply-chain-compromise-anatomy-self-propagating-worm/)), and requests for "minimum release age" install cooldowns gathered **333, 313 and 181 👍** on VS Code, bun and npm ([vscode #316867](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/316867); [bun #22679](https://github.com/oven-sh/bun/issues/22679); [npm/cli #8570](https://github.com/npm/cli/issues/8570)). But Socket's firewall is free and its free tier rose to 10,000 dependencies ([usagepricing](https://usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/socket-2026-07-29-free-tier-dependency-limit)), and a security tool that misses a malicious package carries liability.

**The middle and bottom of the table each fail one gate.** The agent skill pack is the fastest to build, and ClaudeKit's $49 kit shows **$24K–$83K lifetime** depending on the tracker ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit)), but ClaudeKit sells from its own website, Anthropic's free plugin marketplace already lists **314 plugins** ([Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/best-claude-code-skills-2026)), and testing a skill properly means running paid agent sessions. The PC migration tool has the sharpest timing of any idea, because Microsoft removed Windows 11's local PC-to-PC migration in October 2026 and pushed users to OneDrive-based backup ([Windows Latest](https://windowslatest.com/2026/11/02/microsoft-doesnt-care-if-you-hate-onedrive-kills-windows-11s-best-local-way-to-move-to-a-new-pc-and-only-gives-you-5gb-free)) while the incumbent PCmover costs **$59.95** and draws complaints ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/195250/PCmover/reviews/)); but it is Windows-only, so it cannot be tested here, and the risk of losing users' data makes it the worst fit for low support. Elgato pays Stream Deck plugin makers 70% ([Elgato docs](https://docs.elgato.com/monetization/revenue-share)), but a watchdog plugin would sell for $5–10 into a small market. Steam's ceiling is real but outcomes swing wildly: Lossless Scaling at **$6.99** is modelled at **$7.56M** gross by one estimator and **$19.05M** by another ([Steam Revenue Calculator](https://steam-revenue-calculator.com/app/993090/lossless-scaling); [VaporLens](https://vaporlens.app/app/993090/lossless_scaling/stats/details)), viral Bongo Cat netted only **$2,000–$4,050 a month** ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/viral-steam-hit-bongo-cat-doesnt-actually-make-any-money/1100-6532777/)), and the free SCSKiller drew **4,662 GitHub stars in about seven weeks**, which shows both the demand and the free competition ([GitHub](https://github.com/BlueHeisenberg/SCSKiller)).

## Blender 5 broke 116 add-ons, and Superhive buyers still pay for tools

The demand case for the winner rests on Blender's own breakage data. Version 5.0 removed `scene.node_tree` and the legacy Action API and moved Grease Pencil to the v3 data model ([Blender 5.0 Python API notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/python_api/)), and 5.1 then moved to Python 3.13 ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/blender-5-1-addon-compatibility-python-313-guide)). Before 5.0 shipped, Blender's extensions team estimated that **123 of 612 hosted add-ons (about 20%)** called APIs slated for removal ([projects.blender.org #364](https://projects.blender.org/infrastructure/extensions-website/issues/364)); after release, a devtalk survey counted **116 affected add-ons, 43 marked broken**, and **about 30% still at least partly broken "a couple versions after"** ([devtalk survey](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/survery-of-impacted-addons-by-breaking-python-api-changes/46289)). In the Grease Pencil niche specifically, the paid G-Mesh tool, which turns strokes into mesh, is "supported up to Blender 4.0" with "no plans for future updates" ([Superhive G-Mesh](https://superhivemarket.com/products/g-mesh/)). Its existence shows buyers once paid for exactly this job, and its abandonment leaves them stranded on Blender 5.x. The nearest paid alternative, RetopoFlow 4, is general retopology rather than a Grease Pencil tool ([Ranch Computing](https://www.ranchcomputing.com/en/best-plugins-for-blender/)). This is **indirect** evidence, because no thread counting "please make this" requests was retrievable, which is why validation step 1 is mandatory rather than optional.

Superhive satisfies the marketplace constraint better than any alternative. It reports **7,500 creators, 60,000+ products and nearly $50M paid out since 2014** ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/we-built-this-together-now-we-need-to-fix-it)), and its top sellers are tools: Auto-Rig Pro sells at $25 Lite or $50 Full with **448 ratings** ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/auto-rig-pro)), FLIP Fluids at **$79** ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/flipfluids)), and the Hard Ops/BoxCutter bundle at **$38** ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/hard-ops--boxcutter-ultimate-bundle)). Creators keep "up to 90%" on paid creator subscriptions ([Superhive pricing](https://superhivemarket.com/pricing)), while forum and third-party reports put the free tier near 70% ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/superhive-changes/1634680?page=21)). Add-ons must be GPL-compatible, so buyers may legally share the code and sales rest on convenience, updates and support rather than DRM. Superhive's **June 2026 Support Periods** turn that maintenance into recurring revenue: buyers get 12 months of updates and support, then renew at a discount that FLIP Fluids sets at 25% of the current price and BlenderNation reported as 50% ([Superhive FAQ](https://support.superhivemarket.com/article/331-support-period-faqs-creators); [FLIP Fluids FAQ](https://github.com/rlguy/Blender-FLIP-Fluids/wiki/Frequently-Asked-Questions); [BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2026/04/01/incoming-policy-changes-on-superhive/)). Blendkit now also accepts paid add-ons packaged as Blender extensions, which adds a second channel ([Blendkit](https://www.blendkit.com/add-ons-upload-beta/)).

**Expect hundreds of dollars in year one, not thousands a month.** One creator estimates that a tool from a seller with no audience earns **about $50 a month** and that the big earners have YouTube followings ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/superhive-changes/1634680?page=21)). The 2024 Cyber Sale paid creators $540,903, but the average per creator fell to **$375.11** from $523.41 ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/blender-market-2024-cyber-sale-recap)), and the six-day 2025 Winter Sale paid 1,483 creators $354,858, **about $239 each** ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/winter-sale-2025-recap)). Those averages include the top sellers, so a typical newcomer earns less, and Superhive's founder says creators are "squeezed between declining sales and an ever-increasing number of customers who expect ongoing updates and support" ([Autotroph](https://autotroph.com/the-blender-creator-economy-is-breaking-lets-start-talking-about-it)). My inference is that one add-on plus a couple of sale events lands in the high hundreds to low thousands of dollars in year one; at roughly $16.80 net per $24 sale, clearing $1,000 takes about 60 sales. The ceiling is real but slow, since two creators had passed **$1M lifetime** by 2022 ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/2022-wrap-up-and-cyber-results?page=2)).

Your real edge is cheap maintenance, which is exactly where incumbents fail. The founder's complaint and the 30% still-broken rate describe a market where buyers are repeatedly burned by abandonment. An AI agent running headless `bpy` tests against every 5.x release can keep a "tested on every Blender release" promise for almost nothing, and Support Period renewals now pay for that promise, so Blender's API churn hurts competing sellers while it works in your favour.

## A two-week v1 at $24, sold with no website

### Scope: rule-checkable conversion first, hard retopology later

V1 ships only the parts whose output a test can verify by rule, and defers robust automatic quad retopology, which the marketplace analysis flagged as the main technical risk.

| Area | In v1 | Deferred to v1.1+ |
|---|---|---|
| Strokes → mesh | Flat ribbons or round tubes; width follows stroke radius and pressure; even resampling into clean quad strips with end caps | Fully automatic quad-patch retopology from sketches |
| Fills → mesh | Filled shapes become faces, holes included, with a triangulated or quad-dominant option | Smart edge flow across multiple fills |
| Attributes | Stroke material and vertex colour carry over to mesh materials and colour attributes; UVs run along stroke length for textured line art | Baking stroke textures |
| Retopology guide | Project strokes onto a target mesh to lay quad strips on a sculpt | Filling patches between guide strips |
| Workflow | Keeps the source Grease Pencil object; one-click re-convert, merge-by-distance, batch over selected layers | Baking animated frame ranges into mesh sequences |
| Compatibility | Blender 5.0–5.3, Grease Pencil v3 only, with a tested-versions table on the listing | Blender ≤4.2 (the legacy Grease Pencil model) |

**Price it at $24 for one seat and about $59 for up to five seats.** That sits inside the $19–29 band the marketplace analysis recommends, anchored by Auto-Rig Pro Lite at $25 and the $38 Hard Ops bundle, with seat tiers copied from Auto-Rig Pro and FLIP Fluids. Enable Support Periods with renewal at the 25% end of the observed range, which keeps renewing a near-automatic decision and signals that the add-on will be maintained.

### Testing: every feature proven headless before anyone opens Blender

All of v1 is pure Python against `bpy`, so the agent installs the `bpy` 5.3 package in this container, builds Grease Pencil v3 objects from code (straight strokes, spirals, self-intersecting strokes, zero-length strokes, pressure ramps and fills with holes), runs the add-on's operators headless, and asserts geometric properties in pytest. Those assertions check that meshes are closed except where boundaries are intended, that no face has zero area, that the share of quad faces stays above a threshold, that vertex counts stay within tolerance of the resampling spacing, and that colour attributes and UVs exist. Running the same suite against every 5.x `bpy` wheel the package index offers backs the compatibility table on the listing, and the same harness can run Blender's native conversion for the comparison in validation step 1. Only two steps need a desktop with a screen and GPU: recording the demo video and a final manual pass over the user interface.

### Selling: listing, video, sale events, and nothing else

The Superhive listing is the entire web presence. Its title should carry the buyer's search terms ("Grease Pencil," "GPv3," "Blender 5," "stroke to mesh"), with retopology covered in the description, before/after GIFs on the page, and a 60-second demo video uploaded to YouTube and embedded in the listing; since creators with YouTube followings are the ones who sell, that video is the only off-platform asset worth making. Documentation lives in a README inside the zip and in the listing itself, and the add-on ships with no telemetry, license server or landing page, because GPL licensing plus Superhive's delivery makes them unnecessary. Time the paid launch to land just before a Superhive sale event, since Superhive's own figures show these events drive creator earnings while organic discovery for unknown sellers stays weak.

Once the Superhive listing is live, add a Gumroad mirror, where Discover sales cost 30% and direct sales 10% plus 50¢ ([checkoutpage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/gumroad-fees)) and which is also the documented route if you are under 18; list on Blendkit later as a paid extension. A free, ribbon-only "lite" build on extensions.blender.org could act as a discovery funnel inside Blender itself, but that platform requires GPLv3+, forbids paywalls and purchase prompts inside Blender's interface, and allows funding links only in the listing description ([Extensions ToS](https://extensions.blender.org/terms-of-service/)), so confirm that a link to the paid version is permitted before relying on it. If you sell from the US, self-employment tax applies once net earnings reach **$400** in a year ([aibusiness.vc](https://aibusiness.vc/government/taxes-on-ai-side-hustle-income-what-you-owe-2026)).

## Read every figure as a search snippet, not verified data

Every research stream reports that direct page fetches were blocked for the marketplaces that matter most (Superhive, Fab, the Unity Asset Store, JetBrains, Steam and itch.io) and for Reddit and Hacker News, so **every price, review count and revenue figure above was read from search-engine extracts rather than the live page**. Several streams also exhausted their search budget before reaching the marketplaces your constraint emphasises; one logistics stream labels Superhive, Fab, Unity and Atlassian terms "UNVERIFIED," even though another stream later recovered some of them from snippets. Demand evidence is therefore inferred from abandoned products, breakage surveys, GitHub reactions, press coverage and incumbent pricing, and not a single "I would pay for this" quote could be collected.

The specific weak points matter for this decision. Revenue estimates conflict by **2.5–50x** (Lossless Scaling at $7.6M versus $19M; Bongo Cat estimated at $670K but actually netting about $2–4K a month), so always use the low end. Superhive's base commission is not confirmed by an official page, and sources disagree on whether Support Periods are opt-in per product or universal and whether renewal runs 25% or 50%. "No competitor found" for the Grease Pencil and slotted-Actions gaps means none appeared in search results, not a confirmed absence. Minimum seller ages are confirmed only for Gumroad and Apple. Many fee comparisons come from blogs written by competing payment vendors, some Blender data is old (the "workflow tools sell best" observation dates from about 2015, the $1M creator milestones from 2022), and the scoring table is my synthesis across eight research streams rather than the output of any one of them.

## Conclusion

Your constraints invert the usual indie ranking. Chrome extensions, Mac apps sold direct and boilerplates hold the best revenue records, and all of them depend on the seller's own checkout or audience, so the real question becomes which first step is cheapest, safest and most testable rather than which product has the highest ceiling. On that question Blender wins because of where its opportunity comes from, not because of raw demand volume. Platform breakage keeps recurring, from 5.0's API removals to 5.1's Python change, mirroring Unity's Render Graph shift; buyers are visibly burned by abandoned tools; and Superhive's 2026 Support Periods let sellers charge for staying current. An AI agent with headless cross-version tests turns the cost every incumbent complains about into this builder's advantage.

The practical consequence is to think in catalogs rather than single products. Listing assets, the test harness, sale-event timing and the buyer base all carry over to a second Blender 5 add-on, with the slotted-Actions pack already queued, and each future Blender release that breaks other sellers' tools offers another chance to be the maintained option. If you later get a Windows machine to test on, the high-variance Steam utility becomes a sensible second bet; until then, it does not match how this builder works.
